# ZINKO — Editorial direction

This is an editorial system, not a fixed video template. Each video gets its own visual language based on its subject, pace, narration and available footage.

## Core identity (carry across videos)
- Energetic, human-edited pacing: cut on meaning, emphasis, reactions and audio beats; do not cut rapidly just for the sake of speed.
- Real footage first: use the creator's supplied gameplay, camera footage, screenshots, memes and sourced stills. Do not replace real references with synthetic illustrations when authentic material exists.
- Captions: bold yellow words with a strong dark outline/shadow, timed to speech. Emphasize only key words; do not turn every sentence into a giant title.
- Graphic overlays: use labels, arrows, circles, UI callouts, stickers, cut-outs and occasional collage/paper treatments when they clarify a point.
- Camera language: purposeful punch-ins, pull-backs, reframes, tracking moves and brief freeze-frame emphasis. Vary scale and direction; avoid repetitive preset zooms.
- Transitions: prefer motivated cuts, match cuts, whip pans, foreground wipes and sound-led transitions. Use flashy transitions sparingly.
- Sound design: sync impacts, whooshes, clicks, risers and comedic accents to visible actions. Preserve dialogue intelligibility. No background music unless the brief explicitly requests it.
- Depth/3D: add parallax, layered cut-outs, tracked labels and dimensional typography where they support the scene. Avoid decorative 3D that competes with the story.

## Adapt per video
Before editing, classify the content (news/explainer, gaming, comedy, tutorial, story, promo), identify its strongest hook, and build a scene-by-scene visual plan. Do not reuse the same title animation, transition pack or color treatment automatically.

## Editorial workflow
1. Transcribe/mark the narration and identify hook, reveals, pauses and punchlines.
2. Build an edit decision list with source clip, in/out, narration phrase, visual action, caption, sound cue and transition.
3. Select authentic supporting media. Record source URLs and usage/license notes.
4. Rough cut first; verify story clarity and pacing before adding graphics.
5. Add captions, overlays, motion, sound design and color finishing.
6. Render a low-cost preview; inspect the whole result, fix timing/cropping/audio, then render final.
7. Never call a video final until the rendered file has been opened and checked.

## R7 / GTA VI project guardrails
- Keep the creator's voice and narration; do not rewrite dialogue without approval.
- Keep GTA VI facts and dates exactly as stated in the approved script; verify current facts before adding external claims.
- Use supplied gameplay and authentic GTA VI imagery where available. Do not invent GTA footage or imply a fan-made graphic is official Rockstar material.
- Preserve the ZINKO caption language and visual rhythm while varying scene treatments.
- The previous 30-second Remotion R8 render was only a synthetic motion test. It is not the final edit and should not be used as a replacement for the creator's R7 footage.

## Required source package for a real R8 edit
- Final R7 export (reference for timing/style).
- Original narration stem (WAV/MP3) if separate from the export.
- Original video clips and any selected memes/screenshots.
- Sound effects pack.
- Approved script or scene list.
- Optional: project-specific logo/font files.
